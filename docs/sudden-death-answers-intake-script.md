# Sudden Death Answers — Next-of-Kin Intake Script (Draft)

**Covers checklist item N** in `docs/sudden-death-answers-az-todo.md`.
**Status:** Draft for review by clinical/grief-counseling advisor and counsel before staff use. Tone matters as much as content here — this call happens in the worst hours of someone's life.

---

## Before the call
- Confirm the referral source (funeral home, hospital, or direct family contact) and what they already know.
- Confirm the timing window remaining before embalming/cremation/burial (checklist item E) — this affects how much urgency to convey, gently.
- Have the consent forms (`docs/sudden-death-answers-consent-forms.md`) and pricing tiers ready to reference, not to lead with.

## Opening
> "Hi, this is [Name] with TabulaMedica. I understand [funeral home/hospital] reached out because your family is looking for answers about [Decedent's name]'s passing. I'm so sorry for your loss. I want to explain what we can offer, and there's no pressure at all to move forward — I just want you to have the information while there's still time to decide."

## Explaining the service (keep it plain, not sales-y)
> "What we do is look for a medical cause of death without an invasive autopsy. That means the body stays intact, and we can usually complete sample collection and imaging within the time before [burial/cremation]. We look at three things: a scan of the body, a simple blood and cheek-swab test for genetic causes, and a review of any medical records. Sometimes we also do a toxicology test."

## The honest-yield conversation (say this before any pricing)
> "I want to be upfront with you: this doesn't guarantee an answer. Most families do get a probable cause, and about 1 in 5 to 1 in 3 truly unexplained cases turn out to have a specific genetic cause we can identify — which can also matter for other family members' health. But it's not the same as a full autopsy, and there are a few things — like some heart rhythm problems or a blood clot in the lungs — that only a full autopsy could catch for certain. If getting the most certain answer possible matters more to you than avoiding an autopsy, I can also help you understand that option."

## If they want to proceed
1. Confirm authorization (next of kin / executor — see consent form item 1).
2. Confirm ME/coroner release status (consent form item 2) — ask gently: "Has the medical examiner's office said anything about the case, or has the body been released to the funeral home?"
3. Walk through pricing tiers only after the above, and only if asked or once they've indicated interest — do not lead with price.
4. Set expectations on timing: "The scan and toxicology results usually come back within a couple of weeks; genetic results take a bit longer, usually a few weeks to about a month and a half."
5. Explain next steps: who will contact them, when collection will happen, and who to call with questions.

## If they decline or aren't ready
> "That's completely understandable. If you change your mind or have questions later, here's how to reach us [contact info]. Take the time you need."

## Do not
- Do not use urgency language beyond stating the real time window (e.g., no "act now" framing).
- Do not quote a specific resolution probability for their specific case — only the published range.
- Do not discuss pricing before the honest-yield conversation.
- Do not promise insurance coverage or reimbursement.
