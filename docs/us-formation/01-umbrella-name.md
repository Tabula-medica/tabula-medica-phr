# 01 · The umbrella name — Kinbridge Global

**Chosen: `Kinbridge Global`. File as `Kinbridge Global, Inc.`**

*Kin* + *bridge*: the people and the connection between them. It does the umbrella job
without any one programme owning the parent, it survives being read aloud over a phone to a
bank, and it carries the same idea across all four programmes — stories that connect
generations, records that connect patients to their own care, welfare that connects people
to what they are owed, and archives that connect communities to their language.

---

## Why the legal name has to be "Kinbridge Global, Inc."

Delaware requires a corporate name to contain one of:

> association · company · corporation · club · foundation · fund · **incorporated** ·
> institute · society · union · syndicate · limited
>
> (or an abbreviation of one of them, with or without punctuation)

"Global" is not on that list. **"Kinbridge Global" filed on its own would be rejected.**
There is a narrow discretionary waiver, but it applies only to a nonprofit nonstock
corporation that is *also an association of professionals* — which this is not.

Two ways to comply:

| Option | Effect |
|---|---|
| **`Kinbridge Global, Inc.`** *(chosen)* | "Inc." is the abbreviation of "incorporated". Shortest possible fix; the brand stays exactly *Kinbridge Global* in every public context, and the suffix reads as ordinary corporate boilerplate. |
| `Kinbridge Global Foundation` | Also compliant, and "Foundation" signals charity. But it is longer, and "Foundation" carries a private-foundation connotation you do not want, since you are applying as a **public charity** under 509(a)(1). |

Go with **Kinbridge Global, Inc.** Use "Kinbridge Global" everywhere that is not a legal
document.

One more Delaware rule worth knowing: a nonstock corporation's name **may not contain the
word "trust"**. Not an issue here, but it rules out a rename in that direction later.

---

## Clear it before filing — four checks, about thirty minutes

Do all four. The first is a hard gate; the others are risk you are choosing to accept or not.

1. **Delaware name availability** — `icis.corp.delaware.gov/ecorp/namereserv/namereservation.aspx`.
   Search **"Kinbridge"** alone, not the full name — Delaware rejects names that are not
   *distinguishable upon the record*, so a existing "Kinbridge Corp." would block
   "Kinbridge Global, Inc." Availability here says nothing about trademark rights.
   You may reserve for 120 days for $75; if you are filing this week, skip the reservation
   and file directly.
2. **Federal trademark** — `tmsearch.uspto.gov`. Search "Kinbridge" and also "Kin Bridge"
   and "KinBridge" as separate strings. Check International Classes **036** (charitable
   fundraising), **041** (education and entertainment) and **044** (medical services). A
   live mark in one of those for a similar name is a genuine problem; a mark in an unrelated
   class usually is not. Ordinary searching did not surface an obvious US non-profit or
   trademark using this name, which is a good sign but not a clearance opinion.
3. **Existing 501(c)(3)s** — `apps.irs.gov/app/eos`. Not a legal bar, but a same-named
   charity in a similar space costs you donations to confusion for years.
4. **Domain** — the parent needs its own domain, separate from the four programme domains,
   because Google requires the Ad Grants account to sit on a domain the organisation owns.
   Grab `kinbridge.org` if it is free; `kinbridgeglobal.org` otherwise. Take the `.com` too
   if it is cheap, and redirect it — it stops someone else from sitting on your brand.

If step 1 or 2 comes back dirty, the fallbacks that preserve the idea are **Kinbridge
Foundation** or **Kinbridge Institute** — both compliant with the Delaware word rule on
their own, no "Inc." needed.

## The four programme brands are DBAs, not entities

Katha.kids, UHR, Noorjyoti and Unraj operate as assumed names of the single corporation.
Two practical consequences:

**Register the DBAs where required.** Delaware registers trade names at county level
(Prothonotary's office, roughly $25 each). Your operating state will have its own rule. Do
this before you accept a donation made out to a programme brand rather than to Kinbridge
Global, Inc. — otherwise the bank bounces the cheque. Resolution 11 of the organizational
meeting (`05`) adopts the names formally; this is the external registration that follows.

**Trademark them separately from the parent.** USPTO TEAS, $350 per class:

| Mark | Class | Covers |
|---|---|---|
| Kinbridge Global | 036 | charitable fundraising services |
| Katha.kids | 041 | education and entertainment |
| Unraj | 041 | education and entertainment |
| UHR | 042 and 044 | software; medical services |
| Noorjyoti | 045 or 036 | personal and social services |

Roughly $1,750–2,100 for the parent plus four brands at one class each. Not urgent in week
one. If budget forces a choice, protect **Kinbridge Global** and **Katha.kids** first — the
parent because everything else hangs off it, and Katha.kids because a consumer-facing
children's brand is the one most likely to be copied.

## One note on the `.kids` domain

The `.kids` registry imposes child-safety content commitments on registrants as a condition
of registration. They align with what Katha.kids should be doing anyway, and with what
Google Ad Grants requires of your site — but they are contractual obligations, so read them
before building the brand on that domain.
