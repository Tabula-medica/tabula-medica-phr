# 03 · Bylaws

Adopted by the Board of Directors at the organizational meeting (`05`). Bylaws are **not
filed** with Delaware or the IRS — they are internal. But your bank will ask for them, grant
funders will ask for them, and the IRS can ask for them on examination. Keep a signed copy
in the corporate records.

> Have a nonprofit attorney read this before adoption. It is a sound general-purpose draft,
> not a substitute for advice on your specific facts.

---

```
                                BYLAWS
                                  OF
                            Kinbridge Global, Inc.
                 A Delaware Non-Stock, Non-Profit Corporation


ARTICLE I — NAME AND PURPOSE

1.1  Name. The name of the corporation is Kinbridge Global, Inc. (the "Corporation").

1.2  Purpose. The Corporation is organized and operated exclusively for
     charitable and educational purposes within the meaning of Section
     501(c)(3) of the Internal Revenue Code. The Corporation advances
     literacy, health-record access, community welfare, and the preservation
     of language and culture in underserved communities, providing its
     programs free of charge to the people it serves.

1.3  Programs. The Corporation may conduct its activities under one or more
     program names or assumed names adopted by the Board, including
     Katha.kids, UHR, Noorjyoti and Unraj. Such program names are trade names
     of the Corporation and do not constitute separate legal entities.

1.4  Non-Discrimination. The Corporation shall not discriminate on the basis
     of race, color, religion, caste, national origin, sex, gender identity,
     sexual orientation, age, disability, or veteran status in the provision
     of its programs, in the selection of directors and officers, or in
     employment.


ARTICLE II — OFFICES

2.1  Registered Office. The Corporation shall maintain a registered office and
     registered agent in the State of Delaware as required by law.

2.2  Principal Office. The principal office of the Corporation shall be at
     such place as the Board of Directors may determine, within or outside
     the State of Delaware.


ARTICLE III — BOARD OF DIRECTORS

3.1  General Powers. The affairs of the Corporation shall be managed by, or
     under the direction of, its Board of Directors.

3.2  Number. The Board shall consist of not fewer than three (3) and not more
     than fifteen (15) directors. The exact number shall be fixed from time to
     time by resolution of the Board.

3.3  Independence. A majority of the directors then in office shall not be
     related to one another by blood, marriage, domestic partnership, or
     outside business relationship, and shall not be compensated by the
     Corporation as employees or independent contractors.

3.4  Term. Each director shall serve a term of three (3) years and may serve
     consecutive terms. Terms shall be staggered so far as practicable so
     that approximately one-third of the Board is elected each year.

3.5  Election. Directors shall be elected by the affirmative vote of a
     majority of the directors then in office. The Corporation has no members;
     the Board is self-perpetuating.

3.6  Resignation. A director may resign at any time by written notice to the
     Board. The resignation is effective on receipt unless it states a later
     date.

3.7  Removal. A director may be removed, with or without cause, by the
     affirmative vote of two-thirds (2/3) of the directors then in office,
     excluding the director whose removal is under consideration.

3.8  Vacancies. A vacancy, however arising, may be filled by the affirmative
     vote of a majority of the remaining directors, even if less than a
     quorum. A director so elected serves for the unexpired term of the
     predecessor.

3.9  Compensation. Directors shall serve without compensation for their
     service as directors. A director may be reimbursed for reasonable
     expenses incurred in the performance of their duties, and may be
     compensated for services rendered to the Corporation in a capacity other
     than as director only in accordance with Article VII and the
     Conflict of Interest Policy.

3.10 Duties. Each director shall discharge their duties in good faith, with
     the care an ordinarily prudent person in a like position would exercise
     under similar circumstances, and in a manner the director reasonably
     believes to be in the best interests of the Corporation.


ARTICLE IV — MEETINGS OF THE BOARD

4.1  Annual Meeting. The Board shall hold an annual meeting each year at a
     time and place it determines, for the election of directors and officers
     and the transaction of other business.

4.2  Regular Meetings. The Board shall meet not fewer than four (4) times per
     calendar year.

4.3  Special Meetings. Special meetings may be called by the Chair or by any
     two (2) directors.

4.4  Notice. Notice of the time, place and, for special meetings, the purpose
     of each meeting shall be given to each director not fewer than seven (7)
     days in advance for regular meetings and not fewer than two (2) days in
     advance for special meetings. Notice may be given by any written means,
     including electronic mail. A director may waive notice in writing or by
     attending the meeting without objecting to lack of notice.

4.5  Quorum. A majority of the directors then in office constitutes a quorum.

4.6  Voting. Each director has one vote. The act of a majority of directors
     present at a meeting at which a quorum is present is the act of the
     Board, except where these Bylaws or law require a greater vote. Proxy
     voting is not permitted.

4.7  Remote Participation. Directors may participate by telephone, video
     conference or other means by which all participants can hear one another
     simultaneously. Such participation constitutes presence in person.

4.8  Action Without a Meeting. Any action required or permitted to be taken at
     a meeting may be taken without a meeting if all directors then in office
     consent in writing, including by electronic mail. Such consents shall be
     filed with the minutes.

4.9  Minutes. The Secretary shall record minutes of every meeting, including
     the names of those present, all actions taken, and any conflict of
     interest disclosed or abstention recorded. Minutes shall be approved at
     a subsequent meeting and retained permanently.


ARTICLE V — OFFICERS

5.1  Officers. The officers shall be a Chair, a Secretary and a Treasurer, and
     such other officers as the Board may appoint. One person may hold more
     than one office, except that the same person shall not hold both the
     office of Chair and the office of Secretary.

5.2  Election and Term. Officers shall be elected annually by the Board and
     serve until a successor is elected or until earlier resignation or
     removal. An officer may be removed at any time by a majority vote of the
     directors then in office.

5.3  Chair. The Chair presides at meetings of the Board, and has such other
     duties as the Board assigns.

5.4  Secretary. The Secretary is responsible for the minutes, for giving
     notice of meetings, and for maintaining the corporate records, including
     the certificate of incorporation, bylaws, minutes, conflict of interest
     statements, and required governmental filings.

5.5  Treasurer. The Treasurer oversees the financial affairs of the
     Corporation, ensures that accurate books of account are maintained,
     presents financial reports to the Board at least quarterly, and ensures
     that all required tax returns and information returns, including IRS
     Form 990, 990-EZ or 990-N as applicable, are filed on time.

5.6  Executive Director. The Board may appoint an Executive Director, who
     shall be responsible for day-to-day management, shall report to the
     Board, and may be compensated in accordance with Article VII.


ARTICLE VI — COMMITTEES

6.1  Committees. The Board may establish committees. Any committee exercising
     the authority of the Board shall consist solely of directors.

6.2  Advisory Bodies. The Board may establish advisory bodies that include
     persons who are not directors. Such bodies may not exercise the authority
     of the Board.

6.3  Program Advisory Committees. Given the distinct subject matter of the
     Corporation's programs, the Board may establish a separate advisory
     committee for any program. Any program handling personal health
     information or information concerning children shall have an advisory
     body including at least one person with relevant professional expertise.


ARTICLE VII — CONFLICTS OF INTEREST AND COMPENSATION

7.1  Policy. The Board shall adopt and maintain a Conflict of Interest Policy.
     Every director, officer and member of a committee with Board-delegated
     powers shall sign the policy on appointment and annually thereafter.

7.2  Compensation Review. Compensation of any officer, director, employee or
     contractor who is a disqualified person within the meaning of Section
     4958 of the Internal Revenue Code shall be approved in advance by the
     Board or an authorized committee, composed entirely of persons without a
     conflict of interest with respect to the arrangement, relying on
     appropriate comparability data, with the basis for the determination
     documented contemporaneously in the minutes.

7.3  Private Inurement. No part of the net earnings of the Corporation shall
     inure to the benefit of any director, officer or private individual,
     except as reasonable compensation for services actually rendered.


ARTICLE VIII — INDEMNIFICATION

8.1  Indemnification. The Corporation shall indemnify its directors and
     officers to the fullest extent permitted by the General Corporation Law
     of the State of Delaware.

8.2  Insurance. The Corporation may purchase directors' and officers'
     liability insurance.


ARTICLE IX — FINANCIAL ADMINISTRATION

9.1  Fiscal Year. The fiscal year of the Corporation ends on 31 December.

9.2  Contracts and Signatures. The Board may authorize any officer or agent to
     enter into contracts on behalf of the Corporation. Absent such
     authorization, no person may bind the Corporation.

9.3  Checks and Deposits. All funds shall be deposited to the credit of the
     Corporation in accounts designated by the Board. Disbursements above an
     amount fixed by the Board shall require two signatures.

9.4  Gifts. The Board may accept or decline any contribution, gift or bequest.
     The Corporation shall not accept a contribution whose terms would
     restrict its use in a manner inconsistent with its exempt purposes.

9.5  Annual Filings. The Corporation shall file its Delaware annual report by
     1 March each year and its IRS Form 990-series return or notice by the
     fifteenth day of the fifth month following the close of the fiscal year.


ARTICLE X — RECORDS AND DISCLOSURE

10.1 Records. The Corporation shall keep correct and complete books and
     records of account, minutes of Board and committee proceedings, and the
     documents described in Section 5.4.

10.2 Public Disclosure. The Corporation shall make available for public
     inspection its application for exemption and its three most recent annual
     information returns, as required by Section 6104 of the Internal Revenue
     Code.


ARTICLE XI — AMENDMENT

11.1 Amendment. These Bylaws may be amended or repealed, and new Bylaws
     adopted, by the affirmative vote of two-thirds (2/3) of the directors
     then in office at any meeting for which notice of the proposed amendment
     was given.

11.2 Limitation. No amendment shall be effective that would cause the
     Corporation to cease to qualify as an organization described in Section
     501(c)(3) of the Internal Revenue Code.


ARTICLE XII — DISSOLUTION

12.1 Dissolution. Upon dissolution, the assets of the Corporation shall be
     distributed in accordance with Article EIGHTH of the Certificate of
     Incorporation.


                            CERTIFICATION

I certify that I am the duly elected Secretary of Kinbridge Global, Inc., and that the
foregoing Bylaws were adopted by its Board of Directors on the ____ day of
______________, 20____.


                              _________________________________
                              Secretary
```

## The three clauses that matter most, and why

**§3.3 Independence.** Not legally mandatory, but it is what makes public-charity
classification defensible and what sophisticated funders check first. A board of three
relatives reads as a private foundation wearing a public-charity label. Recruit three
genuinely unrelated people.

**§7.2 Compensation review.** This tracks the IRC §4958 rebuttable-presumption procedure:
disinterested approval, comparability data, contemporaneous documentation. Follow it and
the burden shifts to the IRS to prove compensation was unreasonable. Skip it and excess
benefit penalties fall personally on the recipient and on the directors who approved it.

**§9.5 Annual filings.** In the bylaws deliberately, so that failing to file becomes a
governance failure someone is accountable for rather than an oversight. Three consecutive
missed 990-Ns revokes your exemption automatically.
