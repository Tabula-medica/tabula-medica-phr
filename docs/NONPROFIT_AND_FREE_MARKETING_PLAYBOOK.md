# One Charity, Four Brands

**A formation + free-marketing playbook for Katha.kids, UHR, Noorjyoti and Unraj — India and United States**

_Last verified: August 2026. This is an operating playbook, not legal or tax advice. Fees, forms and eligibility rules change; confirm every number with an Indian CA (12A/80G/FCRA) and a US nonprofit attorney before filing._

---

## 0. The decision that saves you two years

**Register one charity. Run four brands as programs inside it.**

Every free-marketing programme worth having — Google Ad Grants, Canva, Microsoft 365, TechSoup, Benevity — validates a **legal entity**, not a brand. One registration covers all four brands. Four registrations means four validations, four audits, four annual filings, and four independent three-year clocks for CSR-1 and FCRA eligibility.

| | One umbrella, four programs | Four separate non-profits |
|---|---|---|
| India registrations | 1 | 4 |
| US 501(c)(3) applications | 1 (\$275–\$600) | 4 (\$1,100–\$2,400) |
| Google Ad Grants accounts | 1 account, 4 campaigns, all four domains | 4 separate applications |
| Annual audit + ROC/990 cost | ₹25k–50k + \$0–1.5k | 4× that, every year |
| Time to CSR-1 / FCRA eligibility | one 3-year clock | four 3-year clocks |
| Legal separation of liability | shared | full |

Unless one programme carries liability the others must be walled off from, the umbrella wins on every axis that matters in the first three years.

### Recommended structure

```
        [ India: Section 8 Company ]  <--  grant agreement  -->  [ US: 501(c)(3) public charity ]
                    |                                                        |
                    +----------------------------+---------------------------+
                                                 |
              +----------------+----------------+-----------------+
              |                |                |                 |
         Katha.kids           UHR           Noorjyoti           Unraj
      children's stories  health records  community welfare  arts & language
         (program/DBA)     (program/DBA)    (program/DBA)    (program/DBA)
```

The four names are **programme brands / DBAs**, not entities. Protect them with trademarks, not incorporations:
- **India** — Form TM-A online, ₹4,500 per class for individuals/startups/small enterprises (₹9,000 otherwise). Class 41 (education/entertainment) for Katha.kids and Unraj; Class 44 (medical) and 42 (software) for UHR; Class 45/41 for Noorjyoti.
- **US** — USPTO TEAS, \$350 per class.

One caution on the `.kids` TLD: the registry imposes child-safety content commitments on registrants. Read them before you build the brand on that domain — they align with what you want anyway, but they are contractual.

## 1. India track — Section 8 Company

### Why Section 8 over Trust or Society

| | Public Charitable Trust | Society (1860 Act) | **Section 8 Company** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Setup time | 7–15 days | 20–30 days | 20–25 working days |
| Setup cost | ₹5k–15k | ₹8k–15k | ₹14k–25k |
| Members needed | 2 trustees | 7 members | 2 directors / shareholders |
| Credibility with CSR + international funders | low | medium | **high** |
| Amendable governing document | very hard | medium | straightforward |
| Ongoing compliance load | light | medium | heavy (ROC, statutory audit) |

You want CSR money, corporate tech partnerships and US grant flows. All three diligence a Section 8 company faster than a trust deed. Pay the extra three weeks.

### Filing sequence

1. **DSC** (digital signature certificate) for each proposed director. ~₹1,500 each, 1–2 days.
2. **Name reservation** — SPICe+ Part A. Section 8 names must end in an approved word: Foundation, Forum, Association, Federation, Chambers, Confederation, Council, Sanstha, Electoral Trust. No "Private Limited".
3. **SPICe+ Part B** with attachments:
   - **INC-13** — Memorandum of Association in the prescribed Section 8 format
   - **INC-14** — declaration by a practising CA/CS/CWA
   - **INC-15** — declaration by each subscriber
   - **AGILE-PRO-S** — EPFO, ESIC, GSTIN (optional), bank account, professional tax
   - Estimated income & expenditure for three years
   Since February 2020 there is **no separate INC-12** for new incorporations — the Section 8 licence issues through SPICe+ as **Form INC-16**, together with the Certificate of Incorporation, PAN and TAN.
4. **Board composition** — minimum two directors; at least one must have stayed in India 182+ days in the previous financial year.
5. **Bank account**, then **NGO Darpan** (NITI Aayog). Free. Needs PAN plus Aadhaar/PAN of three office bearers. Gates government grants and many CSR portals — and TechSoup India validation is far smoother with it.
6. **12A/12AB + 80G** — a single **Form 10A** on `incometax.gov.in`. You receive **provisional** registration valid three years; convert to regular registration via **Form 10AB**, filed within six months of commencing activities or six months before the provisional registration expires, whichever is earlier. 12A exempts the organisation's income; 80G lets Indian donors deduct.
7. **CSR-1** — filed on the MCA portal, no government fee, certified by a CA/CS. **Requires valid 12A and 80G.**
   ⚠️ Rule 4(1) of the CSR Rules also requires an implementing agency to have **an established track record of at least three years** in similar activities (exempt only if the entity was established by the funding company itself or under an Act of Parliament/State legislature). Plan CSR income for **year four**, or act as a sub-implementer under an established agency until then.
8. **FCRA** — required to receive foreign donations. Eligibility: three years of registration, **₹15 lakh spent on core charitable activities over those three years**, an active Darpan ID and a clean record. All foreign contributions must land in the designated **FCRA account at State Bank of India, New Delhi Main Branch, Sansad Marg**.
   Before you clear that bar, the realistic instrument is **FCRA prior permission** — approval for one named donor, one amount, one project. This is the single biggest reason to stand up the US entity in parallel (§3).

### Annual compliance you must budget for

Statutory audit · ITR-7 · audit report in Form 10B or 10BB · AOC-4 and MGT-7 with the ROC · **Form 10BD** (statement of donations received) and **Form 10BE** (certificate issued to each donor). Miss 10BD and your donors silently lose their 80G deduction — the fastest way to lose a repeat donor.

## 2. US track — the fastest route to the free stack

The sequencing insight most people miss: **Goodstack (formerly Percent), TechSoup, Canva and Microsoft all validate against an IRS determination letter.** A fiscal sponsor gets you tax-deductible donations in about two weeks, but a fiscally sponsored *project* generally cannot get its own Google Ad Grant — the grant attaches to an EIN with its own 501(c)(3) determination.

So run both, in parallel.

### Bridge (weeks 1–2): fiscal sponsorship

Model C ("pre-approved grant relationship") keeps you operationally independent; Model A absorbs you into the sponsor. Typical fee 5–10% of funds raised. Established sponsors include Players Philanthropy Fund, Social Impact Commons and TSNE; Fractured Atlas is arts-only and would fit **Unraj** specifically. For India-directed grantmaking, CAF America and Give2Asia act as intermediaries with equivalency-determination machinery already built. Verify current terms and fees directly — they change.

This buys you a legal way to accept US donations while the determination is pending. It does **not** buy you the Ad Grant.

### Main path: your own 501(c)(3)

1. **Incorporate** as a nonprofit corporation in the state where you actually operate (foreign-qualifying a Delaware entity into your home state costs more than it saves for a charity). \$50–\$150.
   The Articles **must** contain an IRS-compliant purpose clause limiting activities to §501(c)(3) purposes **and** a dissolution clause dedicating assets to another exempt organisation. Missing these is the single most common cause of a rejected application.
2. **Bylaws**, a **conflict-of-interest policy** and minutes of the first board meeting. Board of at least three directors, a majority unrelated by blood, marriage or business — this is what makes public-charity classification defensible.
3. **EIN** — Form SS-4 online. Free, issued immediately.
4. **Apply for exemption:**

   | | Form 1023-EZ | Form 1023 (full) |
   |---|---|---|
   | Fee | \$275 | \$600 |
   | Typical decision | 2–6 weeks | 3–9 months |
   | Ceiling | ≤\$50,000 projected gross receipts/yr for 3 years, ≤\$250,000 assets | none |
   | Foreign grantmaking | a flagged risk area | properly documented |

   **Recommendation: file the full Form 1023.** You intend to move money to India. Foreign grantmaking is exactly where a thin 1023-EZ attestation comes back to bite you, and institutional funders, corporate matching-gift portals and Goodstack reviewers all read the determination letter. If you are certain you will stay under \$50k and will not make foreign grants in year one, the EZ is legitimate and much faster — just do not use it to paper over international activity.

   Exemption is **retroactive to the date of incorporation** if you file within **27 months**.
5. **State charitable-solicitation registration** — roughly forty states require it before you solicit donations from their residents, including online. The Unified Registration Statement is defunct; register with each state's charity office. This is the most-skipped US compliance item and the one that generates cease-and-desist letters.
6. **Annual Form 990** (990-N postcard if gross receipts ≤ \$50k; 990-EZ or 990 above that).
7. **Grants to the Indian entity** require **expenditure responsibility**: a written grant agreement restricting use to charitable purposes, donee reporting back to you, and retained records. Do not wire money to a related foreign organisation on a handshake — this is the classic finding in a 990 examination.

## 3. The India ↔ US money bridge

This is your highest-risk area. Three lawful shapes:

- **A — US entity spends directly.** The US charity runs its own programme in India, contracting its own vendors. Clean when it is genuinely the US entity's programme; it starts to look like an FCRA-reportable foreign contribution once you are paying the Indian entity's staff or funding its projects.
- **B — FCRA prior permission.** Available to the Indian entity before the three-year bar. Application names the donor, the amount and the project. Slow but definitive.
- **C — Route via an FCRA-registered partner** who acts as implementing partner while your own FCRA application matures.

Retain an Indian counsel or CA who does FCRA work **before the first dollar moves**. FCRA penalties are the ones that end organisations.

**Apple fee waiver — a concrete reason the US entity matters.** Eligible regions are Australia, Brazil, Canada, mainland China, France, Germany, Israel, Italy, Japan, Mexico, South Korea, the UK and the US. **India is not on the list.** Publish Katha.kids and UHR under the **US 501(c)(3)** to get the waiver — conditional on distributing only free apps and never signing the Paid Applications Agreement.

**Do not waste time on:** AmazonSmile (shut down February 2023). HARO/Connectively (shut down December 2024) — use **Qwoted**, **Featured** or **SourceBottle** for free earned media instead. Meta's charitable giving tools have limited country availability and Indian nonprofits have historically been excluded; plan on organic reach plus a link to Give.do rather than in-app Meta fundraisers.

### Running four brands on one Ad Grant

One Ad Grants account per organisation. You may advertise **multiple domains that the nonprofit owns and that serve its mission** — so: one account, four campaigns, one per brand. Verify each domain in Search Console under the organisation's Google account.

The compliance rules that actually get accounts suspended:
- 5% minimum click-through rate, measured monthly
- \$2.00 maximum CPC bid (Maximize Conversions bidding may exceed it)
- no single-word keywords, no overly generic keywords, no keywords with a quality score below 3
- at least 2 ad groups per campaign, 2 ads per ad group, 2 sitelink extensions
- geo-targeting set; conversion tracking configured with at least one conversion per month
- \$329/day campaign budget cap
- no ads or affiliate links on your own website

An unmanaged Ad Grant is worth zero. Budget four hours a month, or the \$120,000 is notional.

## 5. The 90-day calendar

**Weeks 0–1 — everything gates on the website**
Pick one legal name. Buy the four domains. Ship a real site: mission, programmes, team, contact, privacy policy, HTTPS, mobile — thin sites are the top cause of Ad Grants rejection. File US incorporation. Get the EIN the same day. Send fiscal-sponsor applications. Order Indian DSCs. Claim the free Candid profile.

**Weeks 2–4**
File SPICe+ Part A and B. Adopt bylaws, hold the first board meeting, sign the conflict-of-interest policy. File Form 1023 (or 1023-EZ). Begin state charitable-solicitation registrations.

**Weeks 5–8**
INC-16 licence, Certificate of Incorporation, PAN/TAN, bank account, NGO Darpan. File Form 10A for 12A and 80G. The moment the IRS determination lands: enrol in Google for Nonprofits, Microsoft, Canva and TechSoup the same day.

**Weeks 9–12**
Ad Grants account live with conversion tracking before the first ad runs. Migrate email to Workspace. Enable YouTube Nonprofit. List on Benevity/Goodstack. File CSR-1 once 12A and 80G are in hand. Start the FCRA clock deliberately — log the ₹15 lakh of charitable spend from day one.

## 6. Per-programme playbooks

**Katha.kids — children's stories.** The content *is* the marketing. Weekly stories on YouTube, a WhatsApp Channel and a podcast RSS feed (free on Spotify and Apple Podcasts). Schools and public libraries are zero-cost distribution at real scale. Ad Grants keywords should be long-tail by necessity — "free moral stories for children in Hindi" — since single-word and generic terms are banned anyway.
*Compliance:* COPPA (US, under 13), DPDP Act verifiable parental consent (India, under 18), Google Play Designed for Families / Teacher Approved, Apple Kids Category. All of these mean no third-party ad SDKs, no behavioural advertising and minimal data collection — which happens to be exactly what Ad Grants requires of your website too.

**UHR — health records.** The charitable framing has to be *patients getting free custody of their own records*, and it has to be true. Distribution: hospital and clinic partnerships, patient advocacy groups, health-system conferences, clinical-trial recruitment partners. Publish the apps under the US entity to claim the Apple fee waiver.
*Compliance:* HIPAA where you touch covered entities (execute BAAs), DPDP Act and ABDM alignment in India. See §7 — this programme carries a live governance problem in the current codebase.

**Noorjyoti — community welfare.** Local-first and offline-first. Google Business Profile (free), regional-language WhatsApp groups, Darpan listing for government schemes, district administration partnerships. This is the programme most likely to attract CSR money once the three-year clock matures — start documenting beneficiary numbers now, because CSR diligence asks for three years of them.

**Unraj — arts, culture and language.** Release under Creative Commons and upload to Wikimedia Commons and the Internet Archive; the content then distributes itself and earns links that lift every other programme's SEO. Fractured Atlas is the natural fiscal sponsor if you ever spin this out separately.

## 7. Landmines specific to this codebase

1. **`server/services/ai-fhir-data-monetization-service.ts` and `server/ai-fhir-data-monetization-routes.ts` value and list de-identified patient datasets for sale.** Under a 501(c)(3) that is, at best, unrelated business income (taxable, Form 990-T); at worst it is evidence of a substantial non-exempt purpose and private benefit, which is grounds for denial or revocation. In India it directly threatens 12A/80G — business income must be incidental to the charitable objects and is capped under s.11(4A)/s.13(8).
   Three clean resolutions: **(a)** remove or disable it before you file; **(b)** rewrite it as no-fee research data sharing under ethics-committee/IRB governance, which is genuinely charitable and much stronger in a grant narrative; or **(c)** keep it in a separate for-profit that licenses technology to the charity at arm's length with an independent-director-approved agreement.
   **Decide this before you file Form 1023 and Form 10A.** Amending afterwards costs an order of magnitude more.
2. **`server/services/patient-data-permissions-service.ts` is an asset, not a risk.** Patient-controlled consent is the strongest sentence in your charitable-purpose statement. Lead with it in the 1023 narrative.
3. **Charitable status exempts you from none of it** — not HIPAA, not COPPA, not the DPDP Act, not App Store review.
4. **Private inurement.** No founder may take profit. Salaries are fine, but they must be reasonable, documented and approved by disinterested directors.

## 9. Do these five things today

1. **Pick one legal name** and buy all four domains before someone else does.
2. **Write the charitable-purpose statement** — three sentences covering all four programmes. This exact text goes into the US Articles, the Indian MOA, Form 1023, Form 10A and every grant application you will ever write. Write it once, properly.
3. **Recruit the board** — three unrelated US directors, two Indian directors with at least one resident in India 182+ days.
4. **Publish a real website.** Everything downstream gates on it, and the thin-website rejection is the most common Ad Grants failure.
5. **Book two calls** — an Indian CA who does 12A/80G/FCRA work, and a US nonprofit attorney. Roughly ₹25,000 and \$1,000. Cheapest money in this entire document.
