**Project:** TabulaMedica PHR — new service line candidate
**Status:** Draft v2 (2026-09-07) — refocused on sudden unexplained adult death as a non-invasive alternative to autopsy

## 0. Positioning: sudden unexplained adult death, instead of an autopsy

**The customer moment.** A previously healthy adult, typically 18 to 65, dies suddenly with no obvious reason. The medical examiner either declines the case (recent medical care, plausible natural death) or releases the body without autopsy. The family is left with "presumed cardiac" on the certificate. Over 90% of sudden cardiac deaths happen out of hospital and non-forensic autopsies are rarely performed, so the cause is presumed, not found. Many families also refuse autopsy on religious grounds (Hindu cremation and Muslim burial within 24-48 hours, Orthodox Jewish body-integrity rules), which is exactly the diaspora base in the portfolio.

**Product name:** Sudden Death Answers — Non-Invasive Evaluation (working title).

**What the package includes, and what each part answers:**

| Component | Invasiveness | Answers | Cannot answer |
|---|---|---|---|
| Molecular autopsy (buccal + dried blood spot, cardiac/SUD panel or exome) | None | Inherited arrhythmia, cardiomyopathy, aortopathy, metabolic causes; relatives' risk | Acquired coronary disease, overdose, trauma |
| Postmortem CT, ideally with targeted coronary angiography (PMCTA) | None (scan only) | Coronary occlusion, aortic dissection, hemorrhage, stroke, trauma, tamponade; in a coroner cohort PMCTA gave a cause in 92% with major discrepancy rates no different from autopsy | Pulmonary embolism without contrast, myocarditis, early infarct, channelopathies |
| Needle-only toxicology (vitreous humor and peripheral blood) | Minimal (needle, no incision) | Overdose, drug interactions, alcohol, diabetic ketoacidosis via vitreous glucose; the San Francisco POST SCD study found roughly 40% of presumed sudden cardiac deaths were actually non-arrhythmic, often occult overdose | Chronic exposures beyond the detection window |
| Clinical records review by a cardiologist or pathologist using the TabulaMedica PHR | None | Prior ECG patterns, medication risks, missed symptoms, family history synthesis | Anything undocumented |
| Genetic counseling and family action plan | None | Cascade testing, screening ECG/echo for relatives, insurance timing | |

**Honest resolution rate to publish.** Combining PMCT/PMCTA, needle toxicology, records review, and genetics, the family gets a probable cause in most cases and a definite genetic cause in 13-30% of the truly unexplained ones. Autopsy remains the gold standard for pulmonary embolism, myocarditis, and subtle myocardial disease. The consent form states this plainly.

**Comparison for the family:**

|---|---|---|

| Compatible with 24-48 h burial or cremation | Rarely | Yes, samples and scan done within hours |

|---|---|---|

**Jurisdiction decision tree (must run before anything is collected):**

1. Death reported to the ME/coroner? If the ME accepts jurisdiction, nothing is collected until the ME releases the body or signs a specimen release. Offer the family the genetics and cascade component after release; the ME's autopsy and toxicology results are folded into the report.
2. ME declines or never had jurisdiction (attended death, hospice, physician willing to certify)? Family or executor signs authorization. Funeral home collects buccal and DBS at intake, phlebotomist or funeral director with state-permitted scope draws vitreous and peripheral blood by needle, body goes to the imaging partner for PMCT before embalming.
3. Family refuses all invasive steps? Buccal + DBS + PMCT only. No needle.

**Timing standard:** collection within 24 hours of arrival at the funeral home, always before embalming, and the scan slot booked at the same time as the transfer. This is the only way to serve families with same-day or next-day religious burial or cremation.

**Channel:** funeral homes serving Indian, Pakistani, Bangladeshi, Chinese, and Jewish communities in the launch metros first, because they already field the "we don't want an autopsy but we want to know why" question. Hospice and cardiology practices second. Family-facing copy in Hindi, Tamil, Telugu, Punjabi, Urdu, and Simplified Chinese.

## 1. What DNA can and cannot tell you about cause of death

| DNA CAN reveal | DNA CANNOT reveal |
|---|---|
| Inherited arrhythmia syndromes (long QT, Brugada, CPVT) | Trauma, drowning, asphyxia |
| Cardiomyopathies (HCM, DCM, ARVC) | Infection (needs microbiology or metagenomics) |
| Aortopathies (Marfan, Loeys-Dietz, familial aortic dissection) | Overdose or poisoning (needs toxicology) |
| Familial hypercholesterolemia, thrombophilia | Stroke or MI from acquired atherosclerosis alone |
| Metabolic disorders (fatty-acid oxidation, SIDS/SUDC contributors) | The *legal* cause and manner of death |
| SUDEP-associated epilepsy genes | Anything in an embalmed or cremated body |
| Pharmacogenomic drug-metabolism risk (e.g., CYP2D6, CYP2C19) that reframes a "therapeutic" drug level | |

**Key positioning rule:** the product is a *genetic cause-of-death evaluation* and *family risk report*, not a death certificate. Only a medical examiner, coroner, or attending physician certifies cause of death. The family value is the answer to "why" plus a 50% first-degree-relative risk that is now actionable.

**Diagnostic yield to quote honestly:**

| Cohort | Pathogenic / likely pathogenic yield |
|---|---|
| Autopsy-negative sudden death, age 1-40 (SADS) | 13-30% |
| Athletes with sudden cardiac death | ~17% |
| Sudden cardiac death with equivocal autopsy | 20-40% |
| Including variants of uncertain significance (do NOT sell on this number) | 60-70% |

## 2. Non-invasive sample collection (the operational core)

DNA quality drives everything. The window closes at embalming (formaldehyde cross-links DNA) and at cremation (total loss).

| Sample | Invasiveness | DNA quality | Window after death | Collector |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Buccal swab (2 x flocked swabs) | None | Good if collected < 48-72 h, refrigerated | 0-3 days | Funeral director, nurse, family with kit |
| Dried blood spot (finger/heel stick on FTA card) | Minimal (skin prick) | Excellent, room-temp stable for years | 0-7 days before embalming | Funeral director, hospice nurse |
| Peripheral blood, EDTA tube (venipuncture) | Minimal | Gold standard | 0-3 days | Nurse, phlebotomist, ME staff |
| Hair with root bulbs (20+ hairs) | None | Fair, low yield | Weeks | Family |
| Nail clippings | None | Fair, low yield | Weeks | Family |
| Retained newborn screening blood spot | None (already banked) | Good | Years (state-dependent retention) | Request via state lab |
| Archived pathology block (FFPE) | None (already banked) | Degraded, panel-only | Years | Hospital pathology |
| Banked blood from hospital lab (pre-death draws) | None | Excellent | Days (labs discard at 7-30 days) | Hospital lab, act fast |

**Kit spec (ship to funeral homes and hospices in bulk):** 2 flocked swabs, 1 FTA card with lancet, chain-of-custody form, next-of-kin authorization form, prepaid return mailer, QR code to the TabulaMedica intake flow. Landed cost per kit: $18-30.

**Companion non-invasive modality:** postmortem CT (PMCT, "virtual autopsy") is the imaging partner. It answers trauma, hemorrhage, aortic rupture, and some structural heart questions that DNA cannot. Price in the US private market is roughly comparable to a private autopsy floor of $3,500 and is not widely available. Offer it as a referral partnership, not a build.

Some medical examiner offices will fund testing under NIJ or state SUDC programs; treat those as B2B accounts.

## 4. Regulatory and legal checklist

| Item | Requirement | Path |
|---|---|---|
| Lab testing | CLIA-certified, ideally CAP-accredited lab; state licenses for NY, CA, PA, MD, RI, FL specimens | Contract a reference lab at launch (Labcorp/Invitae, GeneDx, Blueprint Genetics, Fulgent, Baylor Genetics, Mayo). Do not build a wet lab in year 1. |
| FDA | LDT rule vacated 31 Mar 2025 (E.D. Tex., ACLA v. FDA); FDA did not appeal; LDTs are under CLIA and FDA enforcement discretion. Enhancing CLIA Act of 2026 is pending, monitor. | No FDA submission needed for a partner-lab LDT. |
| Authorization to test | Next of kin or personal representative (executor) authorizes; if death is under ME/coroner jurisdiction, the ME controls the specimen and must release it | Two intake paths: family-direct and ME-release. Attorney-reviewed authorization form per state. |
| HIPAA | Decedent PHI protected 50 years; personal representative has access rights; BAAs with lab, kit vendor, cloud | TabulaMedica already runs HIPAA controls (see HIPAA_DEPLOYMENT_GUIDE.md). Extend BAA set. |
| Genetic counseling | ACMG 2023 postmortem statement and HRS/EHRA guidance expect pre- and post-test counseling; several states license genetic counselors | Employ or contract licensed GCs; telehealth across state lines requires per-state licensure check. |
| GINA gaps | GINA does not cover life, disability, or long-term-care insurance for relatives | Mandatory disclosure in counseling and consent. |
| Chain of custody | Not forensic-grade unless requested; keep a documented chain anyway for ME and litigation use | Kit form plus barcode scan at each handoff. |
| Data retention | State-specific specimen and record retention (CLIA: 2 years for reports, 10 years for some records) | Store raw FASTQ/BAM in TabulaMedica vault; family owns re-analysis rights. |
| Funeral home partners | Some states restrict who may draw blood from a decedent; swabs and DBS are generally permitted | Confirm per launch state; start with buccal + DBS only. |

## 5. Go-to-market (asset-light, 120-day launch)

**Model:** TabulaMedica is the front door, counselor, and permanent record; a CLIA reference lab does the sequencing. This keeps launch capital under $300k and lets pricing be tested before any lab build.

| Phase | Days | Deliverables |
|---|---|---|
| 0. Legal + partners | 1-30 | Reference lab MSA and BAA; medical director (MD geneticist or cardiologist, 0.2 FTE); state-specific consent forms; malpractice and E&O coverage; GC contract (2 part-time) |
| 1. Product | 15-60 | Intake flow in TabulaMedica (family or ME path), kit ordering, order tracking, result delivery into the family PHR, cascade-test ordering for relatives, consent and disclosure screens |
| 2. Channel seeding | 30-90 | 50 funeral homes and 10 hospices in 2 metro areas stocked with kits; 5 ME/coroner offices as referral or B2B accounts; cardiogenetics clinics as cascade partners |
| 3. Soft launch | 60-120 | 25 paid index cases, NPS and time-to-result tracked, first cascade conversions |
| 4. Scale | 120+ | National funeral-home distributor deal; SIDS/SUDC and SADS foundation partnerships; ME state contracts |

**Launch budget (asset-light):**

| Category | Estimate |
|---|---|
| Legal, regulatory, consent forms, state licensure review | $40k-70k |
| Medical director and GC ramp (6 months) | $60k-90k |
| Product build inside TabulaMedica (intake, kit, results, cascade) | $50k-90k |
| Kit inventory (1,000 units) and fulfillment setup | $25k-35k |
| Insurance (malpractice, E&O, cyber) | $15k-30k |
| Channel marketing (funeral, hospice, ME, foundations) | $30k-50k |
| **Total** | **$220k-365k** |

Building an in-house CLIA lab later: $1.5M-5M capex plus 12-18 months for CLIA and CAP, only justified above ~3,000 cases per year.

**Demand anchors (US):** ~3.1M deaths per year; roughly 300k-400k sudden cardiac deaths; ~3,400 SIDS deaths; ~3,000 SUDEP deaths; autopsy-negative young sudden deaths in the low thousands. Non-forensic autopsy rate is under 8%, so most families never get an answer. Target segment year 1: families of decedents under 50 with sudden or unexplained death, plus ME offices.

**Diaspora angle (fits the portfolio):** South Asian populations carry the MYBPC3 25-bp deletion at ~4% (hypertrophic cardiomyopathy and heart-failure risk); East Asian populations show higher Brugada-pattern prevalence. Messaging in Hindi, Tamil, Telugu, Punjabi, and Simplified Chinese through Universal Health Radio and Uninsurance channels is a differentiated, low-cost acquisition path. Keep PIPL out of scope: US-resident families only in year 1.

## 6. How it plugs into TabulaMedica PHR

- **Family graph:** index case attaches to the deceased member's record; cascade results attach to each relative with a shared "familial variant" object.
- **Legacy vault:** raw sequence data stored under the decedent's record with personal-representative access, re-analysis every 2-3 years as gene evidence changes.
- **Cross-project:** Sawd Finance legacy vault and dead-man-switch flows can carry the "genetic legacy report" as an estate document.
- **Coding:** ICD-10 R99 (ill-defined cause), R96.0 (instantaneous death), I46.1 (sudden cardiac death), R95 (SIDS); Z84.81 / Z15 family-history codes for cascade orders.

## 7. Risks and mitigations

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Sample degraded (embalmed, delayed) | Funeral-home collection at intake, before embalming; DBS preferred; reject-and-refund policy |
| Uninformative result (70-85% of cases) | Set expectations up front; price includes counseling; "negative does not mean no genetic cause" script |
| VUS over-interpretation harms relatives | Report only P/LP for cascade; VUS listed for re-analysis only |
| ME jurisdiction conflict | ME-release path with signed release; never collect from a body under ME custody without release |
| Insurance discrimination for relatives | GINA-gap disclosure; counsel on timing of life-insurance purchase before testing |
| Reputational risk of "DNA tells cause of death" overclaim | All copy reviewed by medical director; use "genetic evaluation," never "determines cause of death" |
